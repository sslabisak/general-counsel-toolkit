# Outside Counsel Screening

For engaging counsel in a jurisdiction where the company's own counsel is not admitted, or where a matter needs a specialist. Strategy and the main drafting stay in-house. Outside counsel is selected for local knowledge, standing with the court, and execution.

## Before the first call

- [ ] Conflict check information ready: full party names, affiliates, and counsel on the other side
- [ ] One-paragraph matter summary, with no privileged detail
- [ ] The procedural posture and the next deadline
- [ ] Target budget range from the phased budget

## The eleven questions

Ask every candidate the same questions and record the answers in the grid.

1. **Conflicts.** Have you or your firm represented, or been adverse to, any party or affiliate in this matter?
2. **This court.** How many matters have you handled before this court or tribunal in the last three years, and before this judge or arbitrator if assigned?
3. **This kind of dispute.** What is the most similar matter you have handled, and how did it end?
4. **Who does the work.** Who will appear, who will draft, and what are their rates? Will anyone else bill to this matter without our approval?
5. **Division of labor.** We draft the main papers in-house and you review, localize, and file. Have you worked that way, and what do you need from us to be comfortable signing?
6. **Local practice.** What local rules, standing orders, or unwritten practices should shape our first filing?
7. **First 30 days.** What would you do first, and what do you see as the main risk in the current posture?
8. **Fees.** Will you work on a flat fee or a capped fee by phase? What is included, and what triggers a conversation about more?
9. **Budget discipline.** How will you tell us before a phase runs over, and how often will you bill?
10. **Responsiveness.** What is your turnaround for a filing on a short deadline, and who covers when you are in trial?
11. **Outcome view.** Having heard the summary, what result would you consider good, and what would you tell us not to expect?

## Selection memo

Two paragraphs: who, why, and at what budget. Record the runner-up in case the first choice has a conflict later.

## Intake brief for the selected counsel

Send within one business day of engagement.

1. Parties, counsel, court or tribunal, case number, and judge or arbitrator
2. Procedural history and every pending deadline
3. Facts in chronological order, each tied to an exhibit
4. Claims and defenses, with our assessment of each
5. The contract provisions that matter, quoted
6. What we want: the result, the fallback, and what we will not do
7. Division of labor and the approval path for filings
8. Budget by phase and billing instructions
9. Exhibits index and attachments

## Engagement terms to require

- [ ] Written rates and a budget by phase before work starts
- [ ] No staffing changes without approval
- [ ] Monthly invoices with task-level entries
- [ ] Advance notice before a phase budget is exceeded
- [ ] Drafts to in-house counsel at least two business days before filing, unless the deadline prevents it
- [ ] Retainer terms in writing, including when unused funds are returned
