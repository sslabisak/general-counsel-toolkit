# Contract Intake and Playbook

Most contracts do not need a lawyer for every round. They need a clear request, a triage rule, and written positions. This is the skeleton; fill the positions in for your company.

## Part 1: Intake form

One form, one inbox. Nothing starts without it.

## Part 2: Triage

| Tier | Criteria | Path | Target turnaround |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Self-serve | Our template, no changes, under the value threshold | Business owner sends for signature | Same day |
| 2. Playbook | Our template with requested changes that the playbook covers, or a standard inbound NDA | Trained business owner applies the playbook; counsel spot-checks | 2 business days |
| 3. Counsel | Their paper, data or IP terms, value over the threshold, or anything outside the playbook | Counsel reviews and negotiates | 5 business days for a first turn |
| 4. Executive | Uncapped liability, exclusivity, IP assignment, change-of-control terms, or a term longer than three years | Counsel recommends; an executive decides | By agreement |

**How to write a position.** State the rule, the reason in one sentence the business owner can repeat to the counterparty, and the exact fallback language. A fallback without language is not a fallback.

## Part 4: Signature and storage

- [ ] Final version compared against the last reviewed version before signature
- [ ] Signed by an authorized signer only; signature authority list attached to this playbook
- [ ] Fully signed copy filed within one business day, named by counterparty and date
- [ ] Key dates entered in the tracker: renewal, notice deadline, price change, expiry

## Part 5: Review

Each quarter: which clauses were negotiated most, which exceptions were granted, and what the playbook should now say. Positions that are always conceded should be changed; positions that are never challenged should stay.
