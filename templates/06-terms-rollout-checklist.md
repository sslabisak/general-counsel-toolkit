# Terms Rollout Checklist

New customer terms bind existing customers only if the customers had real notice and agreed, and only if the company can prove both later. This checklist covers the drafting gate, the notice, the acceptance, and the record.

## 1. Before drafting is final

- [ ] Read the current terms' own modification clause. It sets the minimum notice and method for this change.
- [ ] Every statement about the product confirmed in writing by the person who knows (engineering, security, finance)
- [ ] Every document in the set uses the same definitions and does not contradict the others
- [ ] Decide which changes need affirmative acceptance (dispute resolution, new uses of data, price) and which can take effect on notice
- [ ] Decide how the change applies to disputes that already exist. Default: it does not.
- [ ] Remove customers in active disputes from the general notice and handle them individually

## 2. Versions

- [ ] Each document has a version identifier and an effective date in its header
- [ ] Prior versions stay available at a stable address
- [ ] The source for each version is kept under version control, with author and date
- [ ] Independent archive capture of the page on the day each version is published

## 3. Notice

- [ ] Sent to the email on every account, from a monitored address, separate from marketing
- [ ] Subject line says terms are changing and when
- [ ] Body summarizes the changes that matter in plain language, and links the new and the current versions
- [ ] States the effective date, what the customer must do, and how to decline
- [ ] States any opt-out right and its deadline
- [ ] Sent at least as far ahead as the current terms require; 30 days is the working minimum
- [ ] In-product notice for the whole notice period
- [ ] Reminder before the effective date
- [ ] Bounced notices followed up by a second channel

## 4. Acceptance

- [ ] New customers: unchecked box or clearly labeled button next to linked terms, at every entry point
- [ ] Existing customers: acceptance screen at first sign-in on or after the effective date
- [ ] No pre-checked boxes, and no acceptance by closing a dialog
- [ ] A way to decline that leads to export and cancellation
- [ ] Programmatic access: acceptance tied to key creation and to each version change

## 5. Record

- [ ] Per acceptance: account, document, version, timestamp, surface, IP address
- [ ] Records are write-once and retained for the life of the account plus the limitation period
- [ ] Screenshot of each notice and acceptance screen as shipped, stored with its version
- [ ] Send log and bounce log for the notice
- [ ] List of opt-outs and objections with dates

## 6. Subscription changes

If price, renewal, or cancellation terms change:

- [ ] Renewal terms shown next to the payment button, not behind a link
- [ ] Confirmation sent with the terms and how to cancel
- [ ] Cancellation available online if signup was online
- [ ] Advance notice of a price change and of the end of any promotional price

## 7. Staff

- [ ] Support script approved in advance; questions beyond it go to counsel
- [ ] Sales and success teams told what changed and what not to promise

## 8. Close

- [ ] Rollout file assembled: documents, notices, logs, screenshots, opt-outs
- [ ] Short written summary of the process, signed by the person who ran it, while memories are fresh
- [ ] Calendar the next full review
